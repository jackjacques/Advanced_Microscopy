\section{Defining the Object}
\paragraph{Q1.1} What would the image of such an object look like on a traditional widefield
microscope? What is the minimum numerical aperture that should allow you to resolve the
nuclei?

The image object is on an actial widefild microscope, a bunch of cells that have a certain thikness and so absorb a certain amount of light.
The image is then genuinely a absorbtion spectrum, depending of the phase.
If $\Delta z \eq0$ (focus):
A standard camera or the human eye detects only optical intensity $I = \vert{}E\vert{}^2 = 1$. The phase perturbations do not generate amplitude contrast when the object plane is conjugate to the detector.

Out of contrast : $\Delta z \neq 0$
Cell boundaries and the refractive index gradient between the nucleus and the cytoplasm become visible as bright and dark diffraction fringes (haloes) around the edges.

For the numerical apperture:
$$R_{\text{nuc}} = 50\,\mu\text{m} \times 0.10 = 5\,\mu\text{m}$$
$$\Leftrightarrow$$
$$D_{\text{nuc}} = 2 \times R_{\text{nuc}} = 10\,\mu\text{m}$$
So, we need to compute the diffraction limits.
To resolve the object of diameter $D_{nuc}$, we must use two differents metrics:
\begin {itemize}
    \item Abbe Resolution Limit:$$d_{\text{min}} = \frac{\lambda}{2 \cdot NA} \le D_{\text{nuc}} \implies NA \ge \frac{\lambda}{2 D_{\text{nuc}}}$$ $$\Leftrightarrow$$ $$NA \ge \frac{0.55\,\mu\text{m}}{2 \times 10\,\mu\text{m}} = \mathbf{0.0275}$$
    \item Rayleigh Criterion:$$r_{\text{Rayleigh}} = 0.61 \frac{\lambda}{NA} \le D_{\text{nuc}} \implies NA \ge 0.61 \frac{\lambda}{D_{\text{nuc}}}$$ $$\Leftrightarrow$$ $$NA \ge 0.61 \times \frac{0.55\,\mu\text{m}}{10\,\mu\text{m}} \approx \mathbf{0.034}$$
\end{itemize}
So, the absolute theoretical lower bound to resolve the nucleus as an individual feature is $NA \approx 0.034$.
